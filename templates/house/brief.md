# {{house}} working brief

Original brief file and date:

## Why this house
What attracted you? What is difficult about the current layout? Describe one ordinary day and one occasion you would like to host.

## People, access and possessions
Who uses the house, now and in the future? What accessibility needs, furniture, equipment, collections or pets should shape the design?

## Priorities
Trace the journeys that matter across rooms. Where does each begin and end, who else uses the space at the same time, and what might they be carrying? Name the rooms affected and record any personal access or reach requirements the design should support.

What must change? What must stay? What would you give up first if costs increased?

## What changes would you consider?

Ask in the homeowner's own terms. “What must stay? What can I try without checking? What would you look at only if there is a clear benefit? What is off the table?” Prefill what they have already told you. Capture their preferred starting point as well as their outer limit. If several changes would work, which would they prefer and why? Record separate answers for relevant changes, such as rearranging, replacing or adding possessions, changing how rooms are used, relocating fittings or services, and altering the building.

A homeowner can protect an item while allowing it to move. Record express permission to compare a particular option and any later selection in the decision log. A room inherits the house position unless the owner gives a more specific instruction.

## Scope and character
Which rooms, outdoor spaces and shared routes should be included? Which materials, colours and references do you love or dislike?

For building or garden changes, record the nation and local authority, site evidence for levels and true north, and any known constraint on the affected part of the plot. Link the evidence and identify the decision that needs it. For a room-only brief, include just the context relevant to that room.

## Money, timing and disruption
Record the comfortable budget, upper limit, what it includes, target dates and whether you plan to live here during the works.

## What you want to receive
Record the purchase decision, homeowner presentation, room views, shopping schedule, costing and architect handover you need. Capture open questions and who can answer them.
