# Briefing a renovation

Start with the decision facing the homeowner. These prompts help fill the working brief as evidence becomes available.

### What to send

| Subject | Useful starting information | What the agent does with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Property and decision | A project name, approximate location or buying market, whether you own it or are considering buying, and the decision you need to make | Sets up the right house, supplier market and audience. An alias is sufficient for the project folder. |
| Sources for the existing house | Rightmove or agent listing link, floor plans for each affected level, particulars, survey, photographs, video, annotated sketch or earlier drawings | Registers the sources, maps rooms and openings, and identifies which dimensions are measured, traced or unknown. |
| Household and routines | Who uses the space, what happens on an ordinary day, what goes wrong now, access needs, pets or equipment | Turns “better layout” into testable activities, such as serving an occupied table while someone enters from the hall. |
| Room scope and theme | Current room, proposed use, adjacent rooms, what stays or goes, and a few things you like or dislike about a reference | Writes a room brief covering character and use. Include halls, stairs, thresholds, gardens or shared zones where the change affects them. |
| Furniture and kit | Items to keep, move, buy, omit or have made. Photos, product links, models, variants, dimensions or an existing spreadsheet all help. | Preserves your choices, checks fit and builds the shopping schedule. You can delegate the whole shortlist. |
| Changes you would consider | What must stay, what we can try, what we should ask about first, and anything off limits. Give exceptions by room, item, cost or disruption. | Records house defaults and room exceptions so the agent tests acceptable options first. |
| Constraints and output | Spend range, priority if costs rise, move-in date, occupancy during works, willingness to move doors or services, and what you want to receive | Distinguishes a furnishing proposal from a reconfiguration and scopes the drawings, visuals, costs and handover. |

If you prefer a brief you can paste and edit, use this:

```text
House name and listing or files:
What I want to decide or change:
Who uses the space and what should work better:
Rooms affected, including where anything moves from and to:
Things to keep, buy, remove or leave for you to choose:
Changes you can explore, changes to ask about first, and anything ruled out:
My preferred starting point, room exceptions, and what would make a bigger change worthwhile:
Theme or references, and what I like about them:
Budget, timing and practical constraints, if known:
What I want back, and where I want to pause:
```

For example, “Keep the kitchen plumbing. You can swap the table and chairs. I would consider moving the utility sink if it frees space for the washer. Upstairs, keep all the built-ins.” The agent can compare tables immediately, checks the benefit before asking about the sink, and respects the upstairs exception. You can change any answer later. If a layout still fails within your limits, the agent shows what happens in daily use and asks which priority, if any, you would reconsider. ### If the starting point is Rightmove

Record the listing URL, date accessed and which plans or photographs were actually available. Identify the front and rear from recognisable features, then map photographs to rooms and viewing directions. Use stated dimensions with their published qualifications. If the link is inaccessible, ask for the particulars, saved plan, listing screenshots or original photographs and carry on with what is available.

For the next viewing, request **the measurement that decides the fit**. For a sofa beside a door, ask for the finished wall length from corner to architrave, the clear opening and swing, and the sofa depth. For a kitchen move, ask for the positions of windows, waste connections, service entries and possible extract routes. Straight-on photos of each wall and reverse views help expose features hidden by furniture. Keep enough adjoining space in the drawing to test the journey. A listing can support a provisional layout. Wall construction, concealed services, exact heights and critical purchase dimensions need their own evidence.

## How agents fill gaps

First preserve the original wording and files. Draft the working brief in the homeowner’s language. Keep four things distinguishable: **observed fabric**, **homeowner decisions**, **agent proposals** and **unknowns**. For each consequential unknown, record which decision it affects, who can resolve it and what would close it.

| Situation | Agent action |
| --- | --- |
| The homeowner delegates an aesthetic choice | Develop a coherent proposal. For “Vice City”, identify which qualities might matter, such as Miami Art Deco forms, colour, evening lighting or game memorabilia. Offer a restrained and a bolder interpretation if the distinction would change purchases. Record the chosen direction. |
| There is no shopping list | Research suitable products in the agreed market and budget. Shortlist alternatives for consequential choices. Carry a provisional size or cost allowance until an exact variant is selected. |
| Evidence is incomplete but the choice is reversible | Sketch the option, state the assumption and test its sensitivity. If either plausible door handing works, keep both results and ask for confirmation at the next viewing. |
| The layout depends on a preference | Ask a concrete question anchored to the room, such as “When seated facing the fireplace, which side should the TV occupy?” Show how the answer changes the layout. |
| Inspiration conflicts with the existing house | Keep the photographed or surveyed feature in existing CAD. Develop a retained layout within the recorded preferences. Explore other options according to the agreed scope and ask before selecting a consequential change. |
| A physical fact could reverse the recommendation | Hold that fit or purchase. Specify the dimension, photo, supplier drawing, survey or professional assessment needed. Continue independent design work. |

A mood image can communicate atmosphere or a preferred relationship. A retailer drawing can establish the dimensions of a particular variant. A measured survey can establish room geometry. Record each source against the question it answers. When the owner changes their mind, date the decision and reopen affected room, product and visual reviews.

## Two ways through the same process

**A Vice City listening room.** Start with how the owner listens, the equipment they own, vinyl storage, seating and whether the room doubles as a guest room. Translate the theme into finishes, furniture and controllable lighting. Research a console that fits the actual turntable with its lid open. Check record access, cable routes, socket positions and the listening seat. The proposed FreeCAD plan and relevant elevation show how it fits. The shopping schedule identifies the exact console, lighting and finishes, with retained speakers counted once. The furnished Blender view and final AI study use the reviewed layout and selected items.

**A kitchen and dining-room swap.** Map both rooms, the hall and the route to the utility or garden. Draw what exists before proposing removals. Show where each displaced activity goes, what storage is gained or lost, and what the new arrangement means for water, waste, extraction and electrics. Test a meal from fridge to prep to table with appliance doors open and diners seated. If moving a doorway helps, compare it with the retained opening on the same plan. List the survey and specialist questions beside the option they affect. Keep unverified work as a cost allowance. Use the same CAD, product, visual and handover reviews as for the listening room.
