# Running a whole-house renovation

Use the house dashboard to answer **what should the owner decide, measure or price next?** Room reviews supply evidence. One shared sewer, stair, structure, service isolation or spend limit can decide several rooms at once.

## Assemble the house on one page

Read the original scope, subsequent selections, existing plans and budget. Walk the plans level by level, including thresholds, garden, access and displaced activities. Give each affected space a room brief or link to the package that covers it. Record any exclusions. Follow a few real routines across room boundaries. Examples include arriving with shopping in rain, hosting eight, getting to a WC at night and reaching the garden during building work.

Start a short table on the dashboard for decisions that cross rooms. One entry can support several room findings. Keep room gate results in the existing registers.

| Decision or package | Rooms and revision | Finding and evidence | Who resolves what next | Spend or issue affected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example only, move the bathroom wall | Bedroom and en-suite, plan R2 | Proposed cupboard and finished wall compete with the bed foot on the same section | Surveyor measures the finished width and soil position, then the owner chooses between the two usable layouts | Hold fitted joinery and sanitaryware selection |

## Choose studies from the decision

| Homeowner question | First comparison or evidence | Deliverable and person who uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Should I buy this house or commission a survey? | Listing compared with visible condition, previous works, title enquiries, services and the buyer's non-negotiables. Identify the unknown that could reverse the purchase decision. | Prioritised viewing and survey brief, range and exclusions for the buyer and solicitor. |
| Where will we get sun, shade, privacy or shelter? | Occupied place, time of use, verified north, levels, boundary heights, neighbouring windows, vegetation and seasonal change. Compare existing and proposed on one basis. | A timed shadow or sightline comparison, the deciding site observation and the owner's preference. |
| What if the garden or entrance floods? | Trace a rainwater path from roof, paving and higher ground to an evidenced outfall. Draw finished levels, threshold, crossfall and access together. Look at the area risk map and site history. | Survey levels or drainage enquiry attached to the proposed threshold before landscaping is priced. |
| Can the building support the proposed use? | Coordinate structure, roof, services, fire and escape, drainage and extract in plan and section. When opening-up is needed, ask the surveyor or designer to scope it, including hazardous materials where relevant. | One measured survey and consultant brief with the footprint, headroom or route each specialist needs to return. |
| Will it feel comfortable year round? | Check room use, fabric condition, insulation build-up, moisture, ventilation, summer shading and winter heating together. Compare alternatives using the same occupancy and weather assumptions. Include acoustic paths, privacy, lighting and controls where relevant. | Decision on the design to develop, plus a specialist calculation or observation if the choice depends on performance. |
| Will this work for everyone who lives here? | Walk real journeys with the intended person's aid, reach, turning space, controls, door forces, landings and night lighting. Include visitors, children, caring or future change where in the brief. | Occupied plan and section, household feedback, any unresolved measurement and review of connected rooms. |
| Can we afford it, and live here while it happens? | Compare packages on the same scope, VAT basis, fees, provisional sums, exclusions and contingency. Trace access, essential services, long-lead orders and temporary facilities through each phase. | Cost range and its largest uncertainty, a comparable quotation enquiry, decisions needed before ordering and a viable occupancy plan. |
| How will we order, maintain and close it out? | Reconcile quantities, selected variants, delivery and installation envelopes. Record substitutions, concealed-work evidence, commissioning, snag ownership and access to things that need maintenance. | Order holds, records for the contractor, and an owner handover with as-built changes, certificates and warranties as supplied. |

## Turn findings into a sequence

The [worked rear-room, tender and bathroom programme](../../../examples/whole-house-changes/README.md) follows one fictional proposal through all three decisions. The same drawing goes to both builders. The cost of staying in the house then feeds back into their comparison.

1. **Feasibility.** State what the current evidence supports, what could invalidate it and which investigations can run together. Check nation, local authority and site constraints for affected interventions. [UK approvals](uk-consents.md)
2. **Coordinated concept.** Put the selected scheme and operated or occupied uses on the same drawings. On a changed dimension, reopen every room, product and service that shares it. Record whether each issue is for a preference, survey, technical design, pricing or order.
3. **Price and phase.** Ask each bidder to price the same scope and list exclusions. Distinguish the aggregate from its component allowances. Show cash needed for temporary living arrangements and the cost consequence of an unresolved option. Agree availability of cooking, WC, washing, heat and secure access during each phase.
4. **Issue and follow through.** Confirm the named recipient can open the actual issue and see the residual risks. If appointed through construction, record agreed variations, impact on price and programme, inspections, completion evidence and defects still to close. On a concept-only commission, hand those future actions to named roles.

Lead a progress update with the first unresolved owner decision, the evidence that can be opened and the work that can proceed independently. Update the house dashboard when a shared assumption or approval changes. Use original research dates for quotations, product prices and legal sources and revisit them when the next decision relies on them.
