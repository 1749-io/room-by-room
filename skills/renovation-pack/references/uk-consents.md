# Which approvals could change this design?

Use this when a buyer asks whether an alteration is feasible, or when a proposal changes the footprint, external appearance, use, structure, services or escape route. Start with the specific intervention and the decision that depends on it. A furniture rearrangement usually ends with the room review.

## Find the right rule set

Establish the nation, address or plot, responsible authority, dwelling type, any attached or common parts and the proposal revision. Use the authority's own register to identify the property and obtain relevant decision notices, conditions and approved drawings. Check the applicable local plan and constraints map, heritage designations, protected trees and any restrictions affecting the particular work. Record the map layer or reference, area covered and date. An unavailable register, an empty search or a neighbour's permission leaves the property question open.

## Follow the intervention through the drawings

1. Describe the existing building, proposed use and dimensions relative to the original building, boundary, levels and adjoining openings where relevant. Identify earlier additions. Compare only options within the homeowner's permissions.
2. Record a **planning enquiry** for the change in footprint, use or external appearance. Identify any applicable permitted-development route, its unverified conditions and the route to a formal determination where needed. Record listed building consent or another separate consent if relevant. [English householder guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/permitted-development-rights-for-householders-technical-guidance/permitted-development-rights-for-householders-technical-guidance) · [Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/advice/your-home/owning-historic-property/listed-building/)
3. Record a **technical enquiry** against the same drawing. For a garage conversion, for example, give the designer the proposed use, floor build-up, threshold, glazing, connection to the house and section. Ask which structural, fire, moisture, ventilation, energy, drainage, access or other requirements apply to this work. Requirements, guidance and the professional's proposed means of satisfying them have different roles. Check applicability to alterations of an existing dwelling as well as the project dates.
4. Check other rights when the intervention touches them. Examples include a lease or covenant, shared access, work near a relevant boundary or structure, or building over an undertaker's sewer. Give the solicitor, surveyor or undertaker the plan and a specific question. In England and Wales the Party Wall etc. Act has its own scope. [Government guidance on affected work](https://www.gov.uk/party-walls-building-works/work-tell-your-neighbour-about)
5. In the house decision log or dashboard, record the intervention and revision, jurisdiction, official sources and date checked, evidence still needed, owner, next action and dependent spend or design. Distinguish a screening result, professional advice, a submitted application, a written decision with conditions, and eventual inspection or completion evidence. Reopen the enquiry if the design or applicable rules change.

## Worked enquiry with a missing decision notice

**Fictional English house.** The buyer wants a home office behind the existing garage door opening. A marketing plan locates the garage. A council index lists the original estate permission. The buyer wants to know whether to commit to new glazing.

| Evidence received | Finding at this point | What happens next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Only the plan and index entry | The proposed frontage is identifiable. The estate permission's parking conditions are unknown. | Obtain the decision notice, approved parking drawing and relevant later decisions. Keep the glazing as an allowance. |
| In this example the notice expressly requires that garage to remain available for parking | The chosen office would displace the parking shown on the approved drawing. | Give the exact condition and proposal to the authority or planning adviser to establish the route. Keep the order open pending the outcome. |
| The building designer has the conversion section | Floor insulation may reduce headroom and change the threshold. | Obtain a coordinated section and technical approval advice while the planning question is open. Recheck furniture, access and cost once the floor level is established. |

An agent can issue a buyer's enquiry sheet at the first row. Only an actual decision for the named site and drawing can support a claim that consent has been granted. Recheck the official pages and local records when relying on them. National links reviewed on 25 November 2026.
