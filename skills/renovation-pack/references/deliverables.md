# Deliverables by audience and stage

## What the reader needs to know

Use the opening page, drawing title block or issue index to identify the house, rooms, recipient, revision and intended decision. Say what the homeowner has selected, what remains open, what to look at first and who acts next. On a single concept sheet a title and a question beside the drawing can do this.

For each material change, show what exists, what is proposed, why it serves the brief and what evidence could change the choice. Refer to the specific sheet, view or schedule row. Include a comparison when it explains a live choice. Choose the content that serves the commission.

- [Homeowner](#homeowner-presentation-and-room-book) needs to understand daily use, decisions, cost and next steps.
- [Buyer](#buyer-feasibility-brief) needs feasibility, cost assumptions and checks for a viewing or survey.
- [Architect or engineer](#architect-design-brief-and-drawings) needs existing and proposed fabric, revisions, interfaces and the question requiring professional design or verification.
- [Decorator or purchaser](#shopping-budget-and-delivery) needs the location, exact surface or item, variant, quantity and approval or order status.

Take scope and priorities from the current brief, choices from the decision log and holds from the room reviews. Trace costs and quantities to the budget and shopping schedule. Read revision IDs from the issued files. Where sources disagree, resolve the discrepancy or identify the affected decision and outstanding check in the issue.

## Buyer feasibility brief

Produce a concise printable decision brief. Show existing condition and source dates, what the buyer wants to change, at least one viable concept where evidence permits, and how the choices affect usable space, garden access and future flexibility. Identify assumptions that could invalidate the concept. Give a viewing checklist with measurements, photographs, records and questions for the agent, owner, surveyor and solicitor. Flag relevant title, lease, access, boundaries, drainage, prior works, planning, energy and condition enquiries. Record any searches actually made. Present acquisition costs if supplied, renovation ranges, fees, contingency, potential temporary accommodation, cash requirement and time to occupation. Leave unverified values explicit. Source any requested valuation or market comparison separately.

## Homeowner presentation and room book

Create a visual presentation plus an offline PDF. Start with the proposed home and explain how it changes everyday life. Pair comparable existing and proposed plans or views, walk from arrival through the house into the garden, show any live options from the same viewpoints, then cover costs, phases and decisions. Include a room-by-room book covering use, access, furniture layouts, finishes, lighting, sockets and controls, heating, ventilation, storage, maintenance and accessibility as relevant. Show day and evening lighting where it changes a decision. Include exterior arrival and garden views, key interiors and an overhead cutaway. Offer a walkthrough or panorama when it helps evaluate movement.

## Architect design brief and drawings

Issue a self-contained PDF with property, revision, brief, priorities, scope, exclusions, budget and open decisions. Provide the source plan and survey status, site context, existing and proposed plans for each affected level, demolition and new-work drawings, relevant elevations, roof plan and critical sections. Include legible titles, units, scale bars where supported, orientation where verified, room names, level references, dimensions and drawing keys. Annotate door swings, occupied furniture clearances, thresholds, pinch points, daylight assumptions, services routes, landscaping and adjacent properties where relevant. Use diagrammatic sheets where evidence supports only a relationship. Include room, finishes, doors/windows, fixture and equipment schedules where they inform the brief. Record structural, drainage, fire, accessibility, energy, moisture, planning and building-control questions as applicable to the jurisdiction. Provide a prioritised survey and consultant brief with owners and decisions affected.

## Models and images

For a full pack, begin Blender work with the first proposed FreeCAD layout. Use simple geometry and named eye-level cameras to compare circulation and sightlines before selecting finishes. When the client is choosing between schemes, render competing options with matching camera positions and lenses. Record any architectural changes in FreeCAD and refresh the Blender scene.

Furnish the proposal using shopping-schedule item IDs and actual selected dimensions where available. Test doors open and closed, tables extended, chairs occupied and pulled out, and equipment operating clearances where relevant. Carry unresolved fit questions into the architect brief.

Issue the furnished editable .blend file, exterior arrival and garden views, key room interiors, an eye-level sequence through the main altered route, and a whole-house cutaway. Use the same verified renders in the homeowner presentation and architect pack. Reopen the delivered scene from its handover folder and test-render a named camera with its textures available.

Supply independently editable existing and proposed FreeCAD files, a Blender scene with accessible dependencies, and reusable exports suited to the recipient. Verify any IFC or DXF handoff by reopening it. Trace each issued visual to an option, CAD revision, camera and image type. Pair the original Blender view with any AI-enhanced variant. Compare windows, doorways, wall lines, ceiling heights, furniture positions, access routes, materials and views beyond glazing. Rework or omit misleading images. Label artistic interpretations and product proxies. Keep a register of source imagery and usage rights.

## Shopping, budget and delivery

When a decorator is a recipient, issue the reviewed surface schedule with manufacturer, named colour, applicable code, finish and decision evidence. List outstanding samples in the homeowner or architect decision brief. Check the revision and surface scope with `check-decorator`. [Colour decisions](colour-decisions.md)

Supply a filterable workbook and portable CSV. For each item include room, item ID, buy/reuse/optional/omit status, decision status, quantity, exact product and variant, dimensions, finishes, supplier and link, dated price, currency, VAT basis, delivery, installation, lead time, stock-check date, alternatives and fit dependencies. Include retained possessions and contractor-supplied items. “Ready to order” requires the selected variant, relevant site measurements and agreed quantity. List unresolved fields explicitly. Buying items requires separate user direction.

Summarise by room, trade, phase and option. Reconcile costs against the same work breakdown to avoid double counting. Distinguish estimates, dated quotes, allowances, exclusions, fees, investigations, contingency and VAT. Show base and option totals and the cost sensitivity to unresolved conditions. Include a programme with dependencies, long-lead orders, services interruptions, occupancy or temporary moves, payment milestones where known, and decisions by latest useful date.

## Handover and later additions

Identify the native file and revision at each model handover. For a deterministic build, identify the recipe, source dimensions and the measurements from the reopened file so another agent can reproduce the check.

Package a start-here index, architect brief, drawing register, models, visual register, editable schedules, source evidence, decision log and open questions. Include a simple transmittal listing revision, intended use and requested response. Keep buyer financing and private household details in the buyer edition unless relevant and authorised. The concept issue should identify subsequent deliverables that depend on professional work. These may include the measured survey, engineering calculations, approvals, coordinated technical specifications, tender returns and construction programme. For an occupied or completed project, include change orders, as-built drawings, commissioning records, warranties, finishes codes and maintenance guidance when supplied.
