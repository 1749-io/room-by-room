# Checking a room layout

1. **Extract intent before modelling.** Compare the homeowner's words, original design concept, existing photographs and shopping decisions. Existing photographs locate the fabric. A concept may also communicate a preferred side, adjacency, facing direction or sightline. Record the source and distinguish explicit instructions from interpretations. Resolve conflicts in favour of the latest client decision and record what changed.
2. **Make each important relationship checkable.** For example, “from the library looking towards the front window, the chair sits on the fireplace side and faces the sofa”. In plan, compare the chair centre with the window centreline and the fireplace wall. An unlabeled “left” or “right” changes meaning when the camera turns. For a room without a reference image, use its brief and intended activities.
3. **Test use in context.** Follow each important journey from its origin through the actual thresholds to its destination. Include the open door leaf, occupied seats, pulled-out chairs or storage operation as relevant. If handing is unknown, test both plausible cases. Record a limiting clearance, the conditions tested and the uncertainty. Keep a partial clearance labelled by its two endpoints. Test meaningful alternatives when a choice makes another activity awkward.
4. **Compare the same view at each handover.** Place a plan and a camera looking in the original direction beside the intent record. Check the visible side, orientation, adjacency, access, scale and framing before dressing Blender or requesting AI. Review each final camera against its Blender source AND the original room intent. Use a reverse view to resolve hidden areas. Record which portions are inferred or outside the frame.
5. **Diagnose and correct the earliest failure.** Missing or reversed design intent belongs in the brief and CAD. Missing architectural fabric belongs in existing CAD. A transfer or camera mismatch belongs in Blender. A change that first appears in the finish image belongs in the AI pass. Carry a newly discovered failure forward as a repeatable check and show that it catches the rejected revision as well as passing the correction.

Where exposure drives the household activity, include the [glazed-room review](glazed-rooms.md) in the occupied layout. Compare the garden view, glare and shading with the doors and controls in operation.

## Walking the room with the household

### Household route stories

| Household request | What the agent checks quietly | When to involve the homeowner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assess a house before buying | Test current journeys with credible occupation and identify listing assumptions. | Report each material pinch or deciding viewing measurement. End at assessment. |
| Keep the dining table and avoid building work | Try permitted furniture moves with people seated. Keep the table and building constraints throughout. | Present the workable move or remaining limitation. Respect a firm refusal of alterations. |
| Move a lounge chair | Recheck the occupied hall route, cabinet and valve access alongside the chair's TV view and drink reach. | Raise a choice only if permitted arrangements leave competing uses. |
| Consider moving a door | Compare the authorised alternatives with the retained case using the same endpoints, occupants and load. | Explain the benefit and disruption and obtain selection before issue. |
| Carry laundry across the house | Compare the complete journey with breakfast occupied and the hall available. Trace any alternative path back onto the plan. | Report a material detour or a simpler existing route. |
| Make a visit work with a walking aid | Use the actual aid and person dimensions. Check the bathroom turn, thresholds, levels and door operation as well as straight width. | Request the measurement that decides the fit. |

Use the FreeCAD skill's route study and the existing `journey` review. Let routine passes stay in the room evidence. A poor current route can be an accepted feature of the house. Recheck a proposed loss of clearance against the purpose of that journey and the recorded limits. Keep an unrelated or already ruled-out intervention out of the options.

### Occupied routines

Start from a routine in the house brief. Describe who is present, what they are carrying or operating, and which adjoining space they need. Use the household's access needs, actual furniture envelopes and evidence for this property when deciding what would be comfortable. An assumption about a person's reach, turning space or a door swing that could change the choice should appear in the review.

| Space | Activity to try in the proposed layout | Journey to trace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kitchen and dining | Unload the open dishwasher into its intended storage while someone prepares food. | Carry a plate from the worktop to an occupied dining table. |
| Lounge or study | Sit, reach a drink or keyboard, see the person or screen and stand up. | Enter while someone else is seated and continue to the next space if there is a through-route. |
| Bedroom or bathroom | Make the bed with its usual furniture in place, or step from the shower and reach the towel. | Walk from the door to the far side of the bed or to the basin with the door in its normal position. |
| Hall, utility or garden | Put down shopping, unload laundry or park the equipment the household uses. | Follow that load from its origin to storage across the threshold with doors and furniture in use. |

Choose scenarios from the actual brief. For a single-entry room, the journey can end at its point of use. Where a journey crosses rooms, reference the same evidence from both records and reopen both if a door or adjacent layout changes. Consider privacy, sightlines, noise, daylight, controls, storage and maintenance where they affect that routine.

For groups of furniture, identify the reference object and the arrangement that supports its use. Check the actual outline, count, individual orientations and distances. At a round table compare facing direction, radius and angular gaps. At a counter check frontage, spacing, end clearance and access. Even spacing is a candidate to test. Document any deliberate offset needed for a door, host or accessible position. Recheck the occupied route after moving the group and compare the plan symbols to the native shape.

## Exploring permitted arrangements

Use a checked, fixed shell for an in-scope unrendered Blender MCP study of movable items. Record the source revision, units, axes, IDs and candidate world poses. Bring the candidate back to its FreeCAD option and verify the occupied room in the reopened native file. For changed fabric, build and measure the option in FreeCAD first. Apply the homeowner’s limits on interventions and visual work.

## Whole-room plan

Check full travel, fittings and fixed projections at overlapping heights using the [opening clearance gate](../../freecad-renovation/references/opening-clearances.md). Inspect the same interfaces in the furnished scene before showing their operation.

Use one drawing at one revision to test a realistic simultaneous use from this room's brief. In a lounge, show occupied seats, a practical place to put a drink, any requested views and the passing route. In a kitchen, show the cook, open appliance, storage destination and another person's route. In a single-use space show the user, the thing they operate and their approach. Select only layers that affect the decision.

Overlay them on the actual CAD plan. Label modelled and assumed dimensions, origin and destination, and the position or action that creates a conflict. A clear path alone cannot establish comfort. Record whether each activity still works with the others taking place. Use a section for height-dependent questions. For a failed interaction, keep the layout on hold and name the change, measurement or homeowner choice needed.

## Recording and releasing the review

When the whole-room plan fails, tell the homeowner which activities conflict and point to the evidence on the drawing. Test the least disruptive permitted change likely to address the conflict, whether it involves furniture, equipment, operating position, timing or room use. For an intervention requiring discussion, show the benefit and ask before modelling it. Compare authorised options on the same scenario, assumptions and scale. If every permitted option falls short, explain the compromise and ask which requirement can flex. Record the answer in the decision log before selecting the scheme. A failed layout establishes the need for redesign; the comparison establishes whether altering the building is justified.

### Seated sightlines

Use this check when the brief calls for a view from a seat, such as watching television. Record the target relative to a fixed feature and the direction the observer faces. In the room register, capture both the intended position and a use check for the people who need the view.

Inventory every position intended for the shared activity before designing. Where the role is ambiguous, record the assumption and ask whether the seat serves viewing, conversation or both if that changes the layout. Include individual chairs and the relevant positions on multi-person furniture. If the homeowner designates a seat for another use, record that decision. In the room’s activity-positions.csv give each activity and position a stable check_id. Record the same ID on one current use row in layout-checks.csv with its observed result and evidence. Attach separate observations for live alternatives.

On the proposed plan mark the target, each user’s natural facing arrow and their actual sightline. Identify forwards from the saved object's functional front. A line to the screen can also be drawn from a backwards-facing chair. Test the required turn from each intended position. Record viewing distance and the turn from the seat's natural facing direction. Use a section at seated eye height to check the screen height and any obstructions. Label assumed screen dimensions and eye heights. Where daylight affects the choice, note the window and the evidence needed to check reflections.

Overlay the in-use door and main walking route. Distinguish a fixed obstruction from a person occasionally walking across the view. Review conversation and access to a table from the same seats. If the activities compete, compare furniture arrangements with the existing entrance first. Where relocation is within the homeowner’s scope, show what it would solve and what it would change in the adjoining space. Ask before adopting it. Record the reviewed plan and section, assumptions and any homeowner choice in the room's checks.

In the room's layout-checks.csv, use `intent` for a spatial preference, `use` for an activity with furniture or equipment in use, `journey` for a continuous route, and `whole-room` for their interaction on the same CAD plan. Link the whole-room row directly to the native drawing or a legible drawing export. Record the source, expectation, observed result, house-relative evidence file and review revision. Add a row for every consequential scenario. Preserve earlier revisions and carry forward applicable checks when revising the room. Use `hold` for an unresolved check and say what evidence or decision would clear it.

Before furnishing, inspect the plan and plain 3D views from directions that reveal the activities reviewed. If the choice depends on a personal preference or a compromise, show the homeowner the affected options and record their decision. Record outstanding survey work and who needs the answer. The workbook verifies current records and evidence paths. The reviewer judges the actual design.
