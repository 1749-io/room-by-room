# What a Room by Room film may show

A film about the toolkit is a claim that the tools work. These rules keep that claim true and keep other people's property out of it.

## Truth

**Every figure comes from a saved result.** Run the named tool on the example house, save the revision, then put the figure on screen. Keep `RUN-FIGURES.md` beside the film with three columns: what is on screen, the value with its unrounded form, and the file it came from. The [Ryecroft table](../../../examples/ryecroft-film/RUN-FIGURES.md) is the pattern. When a rerun changes a figure, change the table first and the card second.

**Round honestly.** Show "35 °C" for 35.3 and "about 1,630 kWh" for 1,631. Keep the unrounded value in the table.

**Name a tool only if it ran.** A call strip under a card is a statement that this tool produced this result. Keep a list of tools that were planned and did not run, and keep them off the screen.

**Record what was drawn.** Chat messages, a listing card, window chrome, the clone bar and captions on build frames are written for the film. List them in `RUN-FIGURES.md` under their own heading.

**Record which agent did the work.** If the window on screen names one agent and another drove the run, say so in `RUN-FIGURES.md`.

**Label AI photographs.** A room photograph made by an image tool is an AI concept. Make it from the model's own camera, review it against that camera, and record the differences. Flag the card "AI concept".

**Show the limits.** A concept is not a survey, a structural design or a compliance check. Call a beam a trial case for a structural engineer. Call costs and durations the agent's allowances. Where a room is on hold or a width is unmeasured, leave it on hold in the film.

**Do not show work finishing instantly when it did not.** A solver run, a simulation and a render take time. A waiting line or a cut is honest. A result that appears as the question ends is not.

**Use an invented product openly.** If the story needs a shop listing, invent the shop, the product, the price and the link, give the link the reserved `.example` domain and record all four as invented.

## Privacy and rights

**Use a fictional house.** Draw its plan yourself. A house traced from a listing cannot be published, however much of the film is built on it.

**Take nothing from a property listing.** No photograph, floor plan, description, price, portal name or logo, estate agent's name or house number. Draw a neutral card where the story needs a listing.

**A real place supplies public data only.** The example may sit on a real road so that LiDAR heights, building outlines, weather and sun angles are real. Show no house number and nothing that identifies a household, and credit each data source under its licence.

**Keep real commissions out.** Nothing under `houses/` from a real household appears in a film: no plan, photograph, quote, name or message. The film's example house record stays in private storage too. Only the pictures cropped for the cards are published.

**Name products in words.** FreeCAD, Blender and the rest belong to their owners. Use no logos, no screenshots of another product's interface passed off as real, and no trade dress.

**Clear every asset.** Synthesise music and sound in the film's own scripts, or record the licence of what you use. Use fonts already on the rendering machine and do not ship them. Record every committed picture in the asset inventory with its origin and rights.

## Before a film leaves the folder

1. Search `channel.json`, the page and every script for names that must not appear: a real address, a portal, a retailer, a model library, a person.
2. Open every picture at full size. Look for file paths, user names, window titles and captions.
3. Check that image metadata holds no location, device or author.
4. Read `RUN-FIGURES.md` against the delivered frames once more.
